Cut list

PieceCutNotes
Lining · brown plaid2 × 17½″ × 15″ Then cut a 3″ square from each bottom corner — four squares in all. Cut with the plaid lines parallel to the top edge.
Interfacing2 × 17½″ × 15″ Mid-weight fusible. This is what stops a soft bag slumping.
Pocket · blush1 × 7″ × 14″ Folds to a finished 6½″ square.
Straps · cream2 × 4″ × 25″ Finish at 1¾″ wide. Interface or line them — the strap carries all the weight.
Half a yard of 42–44″ plaid covers both lining panels side by side. Lay one panel against the finished outer bag first: puffs take more room than flat fabric, and trimming down is easier than starting again.

Order of work

  1. Fuse the interfacing to the wrong side of both lining panels, while they are still flat.
  2. Sew the pocket to one lining panel, 3–4″ down from the top edge. A line of stitching down the middle makes two phone-sized slots.
  3. Sew the lining panels right sides together down both sides and across the bottom — leaving a 5″ gap in the bottom seam to turn everything through later.
  4. Box the corners: pinch each cut-out square so the side seam meets the bottom seam, and sew straight across.
  5. Make the straps and attach them to the outer bag. Pin them first and hold the bag up — an 11″ drop on paper and on your shoulder are different things, and it is the one measurement you cannot change later. Box-stitch each join.
  6. Drop the outer bag inside the lining, right sides together. Match the side seams, and sew right around the top edge.
  7. Pull it all through the gap, close the gap by hand or by machine, push the lining down inside, press the top edge and topstitch right round.
Before it goes on a table: bind or topstitch the raw top edge so it reads finished, and check every strap join. Uneven puffs are the proof it was made by hand — a strap pulling away is not.
