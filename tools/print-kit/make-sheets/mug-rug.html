Mug Rug

Quilted · mug, biscuit and a paperback · about 25 minutes
10″finished wide
7½″finished tall
¼″piecing seam
½″turning seam

Cut list

PieceCutNotes
Top · plain version1 × 11½″ × 9″ One print, quilted. Cut big — quilting draws it in. Trimmed to 11″ × 8½″ after.
Top · patchwork version6 × 4½″ squares Sewn 3 across × 2 down with ¼″ seams — makes 12½″ × 8½″. Trim the extra off the ends. Scraps love this.
Batting1 × 11½″ × 9″ Cotton batting, not polyester — it takes a hot mug without flattening.
Backing1 × 11½″ × 9″ Quilted with the top, then trimmed to match it.
Second backing (no-binding route)1 × 11″ × 8½″ Only for the turn-and-topstitch finish at step 5. Skip it if you are binding.
Binding (optional)1 × 2¼″ × 45″ Joined strips are fine. Only for the bound finish at step 6.

The layout

Sewing it

  1. Make the top. Either use one 11½″ × 9″ print, or sew six 4½″ squares into two rows of three. Press the seams of each row in opposite directions so they nest, then press the whole top flat.
  2. Layer: backing face DOWN, batting, then the top face UP. Smooth it and pin or spray baste.
  3. Quilt it. Diagonal cross-hatch, straight lines about 1″ apart, or stitch just inside each patchwork seam. Quilting is what makes it lie flat rather than curl.
  4. Trim square to 11″ × 8½″, cutting through all three layers. Binding instead? Trim to 10″ × 7½″ and go straight to step 6.
  5. Turn and finish, no binding: lay the second backing piece on the quilted top, right sides together, and sew right round with a ½″ seam, leaving a 3″ gap along one long side. Clip the corners, turn through, press, then topstitch right round — that closes the gap and holds the edge flat. Finished 10″ × 7½″.
  6. Or bind it the traditional way with the 2¼″ strip. It looks crisper and takes ten minutes longer.
  7. Press, and check it sits flat with a mug on it.
Why 10″ × 7½″: a mug takes about 4″, and the rest holds a biscuit, a teaspoon and a paperback laid flat — which is what makes it a reading piece rather than a coaster. It is a generous mug rug; go much past this and it reads as a placemat, which sells for less per hour of work. Cotton batting only — polyester flattens and scorches under a hot mug.
At the table: price a single at $12, or two for $20. At this size the $12 is easy to defend — it is visibly bigger than the little coaster-sized ones people have seen elsewhere. Patchwork ones justify it better still, and they eat the scraps that are too small for anything else. A set of two in a Christmas print, tied with twine, is a $20 gift that needs no wrapping — the same trick as the Cozy Gift Set. In a coffee print it pairs with the cup cozy and the Rose Latte Cloud as a coffee corner on the table.
