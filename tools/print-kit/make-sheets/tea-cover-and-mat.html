⬜ CONFIRM THE ½″ ONCE. It is the only seam that lands on 12″, so it is almost certainly right: 3 × 5″ = 15″, less two ½″ seams = a 13″ panel, less ½″ all round when it is turned = 12″. A ¼″ quilting seam would have finished at 13½″. 📌 The scrunchie pattern uses ½″ too, so this is consistent with how you sew.

Order of work the mat

  1. Lay out the nine 3 × 3 and look at it. Move apart any print that repeats on a diagonal — this is the whole design decision and it takes a minute.
  2. Sew into three rows of three, ½″ seams. Press each row’s seams the opposite way to the row above so the joins nest.
  3. Join the three rows. The panel measures 13″ × 13″. If it doesn’t, cut the backing to whatever it actually is.
  4. Add the heat-resistant batting behind the pieced front. Fusible (Insul-Fleece): press it on from the fabric side, with a cloth and a moderate iron. Not fusible (Insul-Bright): baste it round the edge inside the seam allowance, or quilt through the patch seams.
  5. Front and backing right sides together. Sew all round at ½″, leaving the 3″ gap. Clip the four corners on the diagonal.
  6. Turn, poke the corners out, press hard. Turn the gap’s raw edges in as you press so they sit level with the seam.
  7. Topstitch all round close to the edge — it closes the gap and it is what makes the edge crisp instead of puffy.
⬜ Calculated, not measured: a fat quarter yields 12 squares at 5″ — one mat and three spare. 📌 Nine 5″ squares is what scraps are for, and 5″ is charm-pack size, so a charm pack is a mat with change.
✅ IT IS A TRIVET, NOT A PLACEMAT — SAY SO. The middle layer is heat-resistant batting (Insul-Bright or Insul-Fleece), not plain interfacing, so it genuinely protects the table from a hot pot. That is a listing line, it is a real material cost, and it is half the argument for clearing the $40 floor. A decorative mat and a trivet are not the same product.
⚠️ IT IS A SUPPLY YOU HAVE TO HAVE BOUGHT. Heat-resistant batting is not something the cupboard produces by accident — like the 2″ bow clips, running out stops the job with fabric to spare. Keep a yard in.
Check whether yours is the fusible kind. Insul-Fleece is iron-on; Insul-Bright is not — that one is basted or quilted in, not fused. Press it with a cloth and a moderate iron: there is a reflective film in there and a hot plate straight on it will distort it.
