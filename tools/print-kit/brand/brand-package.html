Dragon Ink and Thread

Handmade heirlooms for everyday wonder

Small-batch handmade sewn goods — totes, bows, scrunchies, pet bandanas, book sleeves and the small soft things that make a kitchen feel looked after. Veteran-owned. Sewn by one pair of hands in San Antonio, Texas. The audience is book lovers and dreamers, and they are invited to join the Nest.

Five rules that are not up for discussion

The name is always “and”, never “&”. Dragon Ink and Thread — in copy, in captions, on paper, everywhere. Artwork that spells it with an ampersand is artwork to correct.
There is ONE tagline: “Handmade heirlooms for everyday wonder.” It is on the tablecloth, which cannot be edited, and in the footer of every page of the site. Nothing else is the tagline. A second one ran in the print kit for weeks and that is worse than having none — two taglines means neither is remembered.
Three marks, each doing one job. They are not three versions of one logo and they must not be unified. Page two says which goes where and why.
Nothing ready-made is “made to order”. Every piece in the shop is already sewn and sitting on a shelf. “Made to order” describes custom work only, and that distinction is worth real money.
No “kitchen table” line. It is not true and it sounds wrong. The phrase is “sewn by me here in San Antonio”.
⚠️ THE ONE FACT ON HERE WITH AN EXPIRY DATE. The business moves to Virginia around 25 November 2026. Every “San Antonio” — on the site, in listings, in the print kit, on the LLC and the sales-tax permit — changes with it. Search for the city name, not for this paragraph.

The marks

There are three, deliberately. Each exists because a different medium made the others unusable. Don’t tidy them into one.

1 · The plush teal dragon — THE logo

This is the logo. Everything digital and everything in colour uses it. He reads entirely through soft tonal shading, which is exactly why he cannot do the other two jobs.
✓ website✓ favicon✓ socials✓ share card ✗ single-colour printing✗ the parchment market kit

2 · The round badge — where a circle is wanted

The same dragon, with the name arched over and the tagline set beneath it — useful for stickers, profile circles and anywhere round. ⚠️ Check the tagline on any badge before it is printed: this is the mark most likely to carry an old one baked into the pixels, where no amount of editing HTML fixes it.
✓ stickers✓ profile images✗ anything under about 1½″

3 · The sleeping green dragon — printed market kit only

Signs, price tags, gift tags. He exists because the parchment palette contains almost no blue — measured: one blue-ish pixel in the whole card — so the teal dragon reads as pasted on there. A recoloured green version of the plush dragon was built and rejected in favour of keeping the two separate.
✓ parchment print kit✗ the website✗ anything teal

4 · The packaging stamp — planned, not yet bought

A one-colour submark for kraft bags, tissue and the back of a thank-you card. A rubber stamp is a single solid colour, and the plush dragon flattened to black becomes a blob — which is the whole reason this is a separate mark.
✓ kraft, tissue, boxes✗ hang tags — too big
⚠️ IF THE STAMP IS ORDERED: 2½″ MINIMUM, AND GET A PROOF BEFORE THE DIE IS CUT. At 1½″ the serif hairlines break up, filigree fills in with ink and scattered dots blot. Ask the maker to thin the finest lines. Deep-etched red rubber, pigment ink not dye — a cheap dye pad on kraft prints patchy grey and looks worse than no branding at all. Budget about $35–50.
Willow is the mascot, not a mark. The teal flower-crown dragon — “Big dreams, small stitches, chaotic magic”. She belongs on social posts and nowhere else. ⚠️ She is AI art: keep her off product listings and anything a customer buys.

⚠️ TWO PARCHMENTS EXIST AND MIXING THEM SHOWS. The card back measures #F6E9DA; the card front — the art the whole kit is built on — measures #f0ddbc, warmer and deeper. The kit uses #f0ddbc. Its corner ornaments are cropped with their own paper and dropped onto flat colour, so the wrong value leaves a visible seam where the crop meets the background. Take every element of one sign from a single source file.
📌 That crop-with-its-own-paper trick is also why the kit needs no transparency. Don’t try to key the art off its gold ground — three attempts failed, and unmultiplying a warm ground pushes recovered colour cold (sage turns mint, dusty pink turns lilac).

Type

Fraunces

Headlines, screen. The serif that carries the brand. Set it at 500–600 weight.
Great Vibes
Script accents only. The tagline, eyebrows, the shop button. Never body copy.

Nunito

Body, everywhere. The one face shared by screen and print.
The print kit and the sewing patterns use different faces, and that is fine. Market signs are Cormorant Garamond over Nunito; the free patterns are Quicksand and Karla. Three systems sounds like drift but isn’t — each was chosen for its medium, and Nunito runs through all of them, which is what holds the family together.

Voice & naming

Cozy cottagecore whimsy, written for book lovers and dreamers. Warm, specific, first person, and honest about trade-offs. It sounds like one maker talking, because it is one.

Sounds right

“Sewn and ready to ship.”
“Sewn by me here in San Antonio.”
“Join the Nest.”
“The prettiest table in the house, if you want it to be.”
“I’d rather see a piece before I give you a number.”

Never

“Made at my kitchen table” — untrue, and it sounds wrong
“Made to order” about anything on the shelf
“Dragon Ink & Thread”
Any second tagline, however warm
An invented review, or a padded review count

Naming a piece

Prints and products — romantasy

Fabrics and the pieces cut from them get ACOTAR-flavoured names, never descriptive ones. A bow and its swatch share a name, so the library and the shop stay one vocabulary.

Lace of Velaris · Sidra Vines · Cauldron Forged · The Storykeeper · Tea with the Suriel

Plain colourways — kitchen-warm

Solids and lace don’t get the romantasy treatment — they get names that sound like something on a shelf you’d want to reach for.

Vanilla Lace · Buttercream · Dark Roast · Blushing Linen · Toffee Windowpane

The promise the prices have to keep

Nobody may commission a thing more cheaply than buying it off the shelf. Every custom floor sits above its ready-made equivalent — bows $13 over $12, sleeves $35 over $28, bandanas $22 over $18, home pieces $40 over $35. It is a sentence the site says out loud, so it has to be literally true.
⚠️ Compare like for like on CONSTRUCTION, not category. A plain bandana and a hand-pieced one are two tiers, and the floor buys the simpler one. This rule fires when a SHOP piece is added, not only when a band is edited — which is exactly when nobody thinks to check.
Reviews are real or they don’t exist. Testimonials are added by hand from something a customer actually wrote. Never invent one and never pad a count — the marked-up rating on the site has to match what is visible on the page, and a single review counted once.
This document restates; it does not decide. Every rule here already lives in CLAUDE.md or TASKS.md. If this page and those ever disagree, they win and this is the file to correct — a brand guide that quietly becomes a second source of truth is the same drift it exists to prevent.
