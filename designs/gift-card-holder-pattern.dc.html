Envelope Gift Card Holder
Beginner Sewing Pattern · Makes 1
Materials
  • Two pieces, 3½in × 4½in — the outside and the lining. Quilting cotton is easiest.
  • Four squares, 3in × 3in — the folded corners. A contrasting print.
  • Matching thread
  • Sewing machine (or a needle and patience)
  • Pins or clips, scissors, iron
No interfacing. No fastening. The folded corners hold the card in on their own.
Key Numbers
Body — cut 23½in × 4½in
Corners — cut 43in × 3in
Seam allowance¼in
Finished size4in × 3in
A gift card is 3⅜in × 2⅛in. That leaves ⅝in of room — enough to slide in, not so much that it falls out.
This is a scrap project, and that is the point
Nothing in this pattern is bigger than 3½in × 4½in. Those are the pieces left over from a bag, a bow or a bandana — the ones that are too small to be anything and too pretty to throw away. Go to the scrap bin before you go to the shop. Six little pieces and you have a present.

The Pieces
Six rectangles, every edge straight. There is nothing to trace and nothing to print at actual size — measure, cut, and you are ready to sew.
Cut 2
3½in × 4½in
One is the front, one is the lining. 4½in is the width — it finishes wider than it is tall.
Cut 4
3in × 3in
Each one folds in half corner to corner and becomes a triangle. Wrong sides together — the fold is on show.
How they sit

Lay the front piece right side up. Set a folded triangle into each corner, raw edges flush with the raw edges of the body and the folded edges pointing in towards the middle.

They will overlap each other in the centre. That overlap is the whole design — the diamond it leaves is where the card goes in.

A 3in square shows almost no pattern, so save a large print for the body and use something small or plain for the corners.

The drawings are not to scale. They are shrunk to fit the page. Always cut to the written inch measurements, never by measuring the picture. Every measurement here includes the ¼in seam allowance, so cut exactly what it says and add nothing.
Sewing It
Nine steps and about half an hour. ¼in seam allowance throughout.
  1. Press all six pieces. They are small, and a crease you sew in stays in.
  2. Fold each 3in square in half on the diagonal, wrong sides together, and press hard. You now have four triangles with one crisp folded edge each.
  3. Lay one body piece right side up. This is the front.
  4. Set a triangle into each corner, raw edges flush with the body’s raw edges, folded edges pointing in to the centre. They will overlap in the middle — that overlap is the diamond. Pin or clip.
  5. Baste right round the outside, just inside the ¼in line, to stop everything shifting. On pieces this small, basting is faster than fighting pins.
  6. Lay the second body piece right side down on top of the lot, edges matching. Sew all four sides at ¼in. There is no gap to leave.
  7. Clip all four corners on the diagonal, close to the stitching but not through it. Four corners on a piece this small is a great deal of bulk.
  8. Turn it right side out through the diamond. This is the clever part — the opening you sewed in is also the way out, so there is no gap to close by hand. Ease the corners square with a chopstick.
  9. Press it flat, rolling the seams so neither fabric shows on the other’s face. Slide a gift card in to check the fit.
Watch for
  • Press the diagonal folds hard. A soft fold makes the diamond look accidental instead of designed, and it is the only decoration on the whole piece.
  • Get the four triangles even. The eye goes straight to the centre.
  • Do not skip the clipping. Unclipped corners turn out rounded and lumpy.
Make it yours
  • Use two different prints for the body and the corners — the contrast is what makes it look deliberate.
  • The lining shows at the edges, so pick something you would happily see.
  • Add a length of narrow ribbon in the top seam before step 6 and it hangs on a tree.
