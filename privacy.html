8. Controls for Do-Not-Track Features

Most web browsers and some mobile operating systems and mobile applications include a Do-Not-Track (“DNT”) feature or setting you can activate to signal your privacy preference not to have data about your online browsing activities monitored and collected. At this stage, no uniform technology standard for recognizing and implementing DNT signals has been finalized. As such, we do not currently respond to DNT browser signals. We do, however, respond to Global Privacy Control (GPC), which is described below. If a further standard for online tracking is adopted that we must follow in the future, we will inform you about that practice in a revised version of this Privacy Notice.

California law requires us to let you know how we respond to web browser DNT signals. There is still no settled standard for the older “DNT” header, so we do not respond to that one.

We do honor Global Privacy Control (GPC). GPC is the newer, recognized signal, and California law treats it as a valid opt-out of the “sharing” described in section 9. If your browser or an extension sends it, our advertising tag (the Pinterest Tag) is never loaded, so nothing is shared with Pinterest. This happens automatically and requires nothing from you.
