# Facebook groups — the reach play

**Written 2026-08-11.** Evergreen, not tied to the August campaign.

## Why groups, specifically

Measured 2026-08-10 (Cloudflare, seven days): **152 visits, ~22/day**. Facebook was **44%**
of them and produced both traffic spikes of the week. Instagram and TikTok sent **zero**
attributable visits — neither allows a clickable link in a post.

But five posts in five days produced only **64 Facebook visits**, because they all reached the
same small following. **The constraint is reach, not effort.** Groups are the only free lever
that puts the shop in front of people who have never heard of it, on the one platform already
proven to send traffic.

---

## The rules that keep you welcome

1. **Read the pinned rules before posting.** Most groups ban outright selling; many have a
   designated promo day. Posting on the wrong day is how you get removed and can't come back.
2. **⚠️ NEVER paste identical text into several groups the same day.** Facebook's spam
   detection keys on repeated content and will quietly suppress reach on *all* of them. Rewrite
   each one, even slightly. This is the single most common way small shops burn their groups.
3. **Participate before you post.** In stricter groups, comment usefully for a week first. A
   first-ever post that's an advert gets removed; the same post from a familiar name doesn't.
4. **Answer questions rather than broadcasting.** Someone asking "where can I get a handmade dog
   bandana" is worth ten posts of your own. Set up alerts for those.
5. **Reply to every comment, fast.** Early engagement decides how far the post travels.
6. **One post per group per week at most.**

## 1. Local San Antonio group — where selling IS allowed

*Local pickup is the genuine advantage here; lead with it. Ends by deferring to the mods, which
costs nothing and buys goodwill.*

> Hi all — I'm Ayla, I sew handmade bits at my kitchen table here in San Antonio. Totes, hair
> bows, scrunchies, dog bandanas and padded book sleeves, mostly in cottagecore and bookish
> prints.
>
> Everything is one of a kind — I make one, and when it's gone it's gone. **Local pickup is
> free** if you're in the area, so you skip the postage entirely.
>
> I also take custom orders: you pick the fabric, I come back with a price, and nothing is
> charged until you've said yes.
>
> https://www.dragoninkandthread.com
>
> (Mods — hope this is okay for the group, happy to take it down if not.)

## 2. The reply — the highest-value post you'll ever make

*Use when someone asks where to find something you make. No link unless they ask; the point is
to sound like a neighbour, not a shop.*

> I actually make these — I'm in San Antonio and sew them at home. Happy to show you what I've
> got if you like, or if you're after something specific I do custom work too.
>
> No pressure at all, just saw your post and thought I'd say hello. 🧵

## 3. Quilting / sewing groups — the repair story

*Sells nothing and asks a real question, which is why it will outperform anything promotional.
Quilters engage hard with repair problems. Post the photo of the damage with it.*

> A customer brought me her quilt this week — worn clean through where the gingham meets the
> ticking, batting coming out of the hole.
>
> I've found a good match for the checked fabric. The striped one is proving harder, and even
> when I find it, brand-new cloth is going to sit against twenty-odd years of washing and look
> obviously new.
>
> So: do you tea-stain a patch to knock the brightness back, or do you let a repair look like a
> repair? I rather like visible mending on something that's clearly been loved — but it's her
> quilt, not mine.

## 4. Bookish / romantasy reader groups — the fabric library

*Asks the group a question about themselves, which is what gets replies. What she does comes out
as context rather than a pitch.*

> A question for the book people: if someone made you a tote in absolutely any fabric you liked,
> what would you pick?
>
> I sew book totes and padded book sleeves, and I keep a shelf of about seventy prints —
> bookshelves, apothecary bottles, coffee cups, one that's just cats. The part nobody expects is
> that they get to choose the fabric.
>
> Genuinely curious what this lot would go for. Someone once asked me for a tote sized to one
> specific hardback, which I loved.

## 5. Dog groups — Ghost does the work

*Pet groups are the strictest about promotion and the most forgiving about a good dog photo.
Photo-led, one line, no link.*

> Ghost, modelling the thing I made him instead of letting me finish the washing.
>
> I sew dog bandanas — this one's patchwork, pieced from scraps. He is unbothered either way.

## What NOT to do

- Don't drop a shop link into a group that hasn't asked for one.
- Don't post the tote line-up or the priced custom-orders graphic into groups — they read as
  adverts, because they are. Those are for the shop page.
- Don't argue with a mod. Removed is recoverable; banned isn't.

## How to tell if it worked

Cloudflare Web Analytics → **Referer**. Group traffic still shows as `facebook.com` or
`m.facebook.com`, so watch for the total climbing above the ~64/week baseline rather than trying
to separate groups from page posts. **The tell is visits on days you didn't post to your own
page.**
