# Market table script — what to say when someone walks up

Written 2026-09-19, at Spring Branch Market Days, the first market. Answers to the questions people
actually ask at a table, in the words to use. **Reusable at every market** — nothing here is dated.

📌 **Venue names and addresses stay OUT of this file** — the repo is public; they live in the private
note. This is about what to *say*, not where.

---

## ⛔ The word to avoid: "boutique"

**Don't call it a boutique.** At Texas markets that word reads as **resale** — bought wholesale, put on
a rack. The moment someone files you there, the thing that justifies $32 for a tote disappears, and
it is the one thing you cannot get back in the same conversation.

**Don't say "crafter" either** — that undersells in the other direction, toward a hobby.

✅ **What you are:** a **one-woman sewing shop**. Handmade. Small-batch. Veteran-owned. San Antonio.

---

## "What do you sell?" — three lengths, because you need all three

**Slowing down but not stopping (5 seconds):**
> "Handmade bows, scrunchies and bags. I sew everything myself."

**They've stopped and picked something up:**
> "I'm a one-woman sewing shop out of San Antonio — bows, scrunchies, pet bandanas, totes and book
> sleeves, all made by me. Veteran-owned."

**Then the line that earns money — say it to everyone who buys anything:**
> "And if you don't see a print you love, I take custom orders. You pick the fabric, I make it."

🔑 **The sentence doing all the work is "I sew everything myself."** It is the one thing a boutique
cannot say, it justifies the prices without you defending them, and it is what makes a stranger
describe you to someone else later.

---

## Why the custom line goes to every single buyer

**Both repeat customers started small.** Linda bought a **$30 tea cover** and came back for a
**$1,050** two-piece commission. Brea bought a tote and a scrunchie, reviewed them five stars, then
commissioned custom bow clips. **A $6 scrunchie in someone's hand is not a $6 sale — it is how a
custom customer starts.**

This is also the argument the campaign has never managed to make online. At a table it makes itself.

## The other questions, and the honest answers

| They ask | Say |
|---|---|
| "Did you make these?" | "Every one. On my machine at home." |
| "How long does a custom take?" | **"About 10 to 14 days once we've agreed the details."** ⚠️ **Quilts are 8–12 weeks** — never quote 10–14 on a quilt. |
| "Can you make one in [fabric]?" | "Yes — I've got a library of 76 fabrics on the website, or bring me your own." |
| "Do you take card?" | "Cash or card, both fine." (Tap to Pay.) |
| "Is there a website?" | "Yes — scan that code. Free local pickup if you're in San Antonio." |
| "Can I get one in a different size?" | Bandanas: **Medium fits a 13″–18″ neck, Large 18″–23″.** Other sizes are a custom order. |

⚠️ **NEVER say "made to order" about anything on the table.** Every piece there is **already sewn** —
that is the whole advantage, and the site says "sewn and ready to ship" for exactly this reason.
**"Made to order" means CUSTOM only.** Keep the two completely separate in conversation.

⚠️ **Lavender sachets are for drawers, closets and under a pillow. NEVER a microwave heat pack.**

## Two rules that outlast the day

- ⚠️ **Nobody goes on the newsletter unless they add themselves.** Point at the Join the Nest QR and
  let them scan it. Writing to a buyer about their own order is fine; enrolling them is not.
- ⚠️ **Never invent a review.** If someone says something lovely, **ask them to leave it** — real
  words only, added by hand to `TESTIMONIALS`.
