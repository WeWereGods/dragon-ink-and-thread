# October organiser — the orphaned $30

A **Facebook/Messenger DM**, not an email. Sent by hand.
**Her name is in the private note, not here** — this repo is public.

## The situation

- **$30 was paid for the Oct 10 booth.** ✅ Paid in full, ahead of time.
- ⛔ **The organiser moved that date**, in her own words: *"not so much cancelled, just had to move
  dates and locations around. The gal in Adkins is not available for October after all."*
- ⛔ **Then she cancelled Oct 31 as well** (2026-10-13): *"I decided to take Halloween off so I can
  take my son out."*
- She offered to **transfer the paid day** to any remaining October, November or December date.
  **Every one of them falls after the Nov 25 move**, so there is nothing to transfer it to.
- ✅ **Oct 30 is separately paid ($30) and still going ahead.** That one is fine.

📌 **The fair outcome is a refund.** The vendor did not withdraw — the organiser changed the dates.

## ⚠️ Why this one needs a different approach

**The refund question has been asked twice and answered neither time.** On both occasions the reply
changed the subject to alternative dates. The last message — *"any update?"* — was **seen on
Wed 2026-10-21 and left unanswered**, six days before this was written.

So the draft below does three things the earlier asks did not:

1. **It asks one binary question** and says a yes or no is fine. A vague ask is easy to leave unread.
2. **It gives the organiser an easy yes that costs her nothing** — see the double-space option below.
3. **It names the deadline** without making it a threat.

⛔ **THE DEADLINE IS REAL: chasing $30 from another state after Nov 25 is the same as writing it off.**

📌 **Keep it warm.** Her register is warm and informal ("Hi mama", "my dear"), **and Oct 30 is still
her market** — the goodwill has to survive Friday. This is not the message to get sharp in.

## ⛔ Not a double space — cash (owner, 2026-10-27)

A double space was considered and **rejected by the owner**, correctly. ⚠️ **More frontage means
hauling more stock, not selling more** — on a 5–11pm Friday night, single-handed, on a borrowed
canopy, when the entire object of the next four weeks is to have **less** to load into the van.
Extra space is a cost dressed as a favour. **Ask for the money.**

## 🔑 The one change that makes this ask work

Both previous asks died the same way: the reply changed the subject to **alternative dates**.

**So the draft closes that door inside the question itself** — it names the offer, thanks her for it,
and explains why it cannot be taken, *before* asking. There is then nothing left to redirect to, and
the only available answers are yes or no.

## ⬜ If there's no answer by Thursday 29th

**Ask her in person on Friday**, at check-in, before setting up. Three days of silence on Messenger
is not refusal — it is a busy organiser. **A friendly face-to-face question gets answered when a DM
does not**, and Friday is the last time these two are in the same place.

⚠️ **If the answer is no, take it and let it go.** $30 is not worth souring the last market, and
there is no future relationship to protect once the van leaves — but there is still a Friday night
to trade through.

## ⬜ Worth knowing before "no" is treated as final

**How this $30 was paid is not recorded anywhere** — the workbook has the fee, not the method. Only
two October/November fees have their route written down (Oct 24 by Venmo, Nov 14 by a Wave link), and
the Oct 10 one is not among them. **It decides whether "no" is actually the end:**

- **Venmo, Zelle or Cash App, sent person-to-person** → ⛔ **no buyer protection at all.** Asking is
  the only route, and a no really is a no.
- **A card, a PayPal goods-and-services payment, or an invoice link like Wave** → there is a
  **dispute window**, and a service paid for but never provided is the clearest kind of claim there is.

📌 **Check the method before deciding how hard to push** — and check it **before Nov 25**, since
dispute windows are typically 60–120 days and the clock started in September.
⚠️ **Do not open a dispute before asking, or before Friday.** A chargeback against someone whose
market you are standing in is a bad trade for $30.

# ✅ ANSWERED 2026-10-31 — SHE SAID YES

Reply came in at **1:38am**, after six days of silence and a third ask. Verbatim, in part:

> *"Yes, it's been pretty hectic, especially in my personal life. I also had to cancel today's event due
> to the weather. Typically there are no refunds but I know you are moving, so I can honor it this time,
> however, if I could get that to you sometime next weekend. Need to collect vendors fees for my upcoming
> event, and as soon as I get CashApp, I'll send over the fee."*

📌 **The ask worked, and it worked for the reason it was rebuilt.** The door to alternative dates was
closed inside the question, so there was nothing left to redirect to — and the answer came back binary.
Third attempt, first answer. ⚠️ **Note what carried it: "I know you are moving."** The move was the
lever, not fairness. Keep that in mind for anything else owed before Nov 25.

## ⚠️ It is a YES with three conditions attached — none of them met yet

1. **Timing: "sometime next weekend"** = **Sat 7 / Sun 8 November**. Inside the Nov 25 deadline, with
   about a fortnight of margin. Acceptable.
2. **Method: Cash App.** ⚠️ Receiving by Cash App is fine; it also means **there is no dispute lever on
   the refund itself** — it is goodwill all the way down, and goodwill that is already extended as an
   exception to a stated no-refund policy. **Do not press it hard.**
3. ⚠️ **It is framed as an exception, not an obligation** — *"typically there are no refunds... I can
   honor it this time."* Which means a chase has to stay warm or it can be withdrawn.

## 🔑 THE ONE ACTION: SEND THE CASH APP HANDLE NOW, UNPROMPTED

*"as soon as I get CashApp, I'll send over the fee"* reads two ways, and **it does not matter which**:

- *"as soon as I get [your] Cash App [tag]"* → ⛔ **the handle is the blocker**, and nobody has sent it.
- *"as soon as I get [the vendor fees via] Cash App"* → the refund is funded out of money she has not
  collected yet, and the handle is the next step regardless.

**Sending it unprompted clears the first reading entirely and saves a round trip on the second.** It is
also the warmest possible reply to a yes: no chasing, just making it easy.

⚠️ **Under the second reading, the refund depends on other vendors paying her.** That is the real risk —
not bad faith, just a payment that is waiting on a payment. It is the kind of promise that quietly does
not happen, which is why the chase date below exists.
