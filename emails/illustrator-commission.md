# Commissioning the January dragon — DM + brief

**Written 2026-09-30.** For the hand-drawn green sewing dragon the January rebrand needs
(art direction lives in TASKS.md under the January rebrand entry).

🗓️ **Send these the week after Sat Nov 14** — the last market. Illustrators take 2–4 weeks plus a
revision round, and December is the move. This is the one piece of January work that doesn't need
her hands, so it runs while she packs.

📩 **Send to three or four people, not one.** Two won't reply, one will be booked. That's normal.

---

## 1. The opening DM

*Short on purpose. Illustrators get a lot of vague "are you taking commissions?" messages with no
money in them, and those get ignored. This one says **paid**, says **what**, and is easy to answer
with a yes or a no.*

> Hi [name] — I really like your line work, especially [name an actual piece]. The [specific
> thing: the way you draw hands / your colour palettes / how your linework stays readable small]
> is exactly the feel I'm after.
>
> I run a small handmade sewing business — Dragon Ink and Thread, veteran-owned, just me — and I'm
> commissioning a new logo illustration for a rebrand in January. **It's a paid commission,
> budget around $[X]**, and I'd need full commercial rights, since it goes on labels, packaging
> and a rubber stamp.
>
> The short version: a friendly green dragon made of patchwork, visibly stitched, sitting and
> sewing. Storybook rather than slick.
>
> Are you open for commissions in November? Happy to send the full brief and my brand guide.

### Why each line is there

- **A specific compliment, not a general one.** "I love your work" reads as a mass message. Naming
  a piece proves you looked.
- **"Paid commission" and a number in the first paragraph.** This is the single biggest thing that
  gets a reply. Asking "what are your rates?" invites a slow answer or none.
- **Commercial rights named up front.** It filters fast — how they answer tells you whether they
  have done commercial work before. ⚠️ **A standard illustration commission often grants PERSONAL
  use only.** This has to be settled before money moves, not after.
- **One line of brief, not ten.** Enough to know if it's their kind of job.
- **Ends on a yes/no question.** Not "let me know what you think."

## 2. ⚠️ The AI reference — handle this carefully

The picture that set the direction is **AI-generated**, and a lot of illustrators will refuse a job
outright if they think they are being asked to trace or clean up AI output. That reaction is real
and it is worth respecting rather than working around.

**Don't lead with it.** Describe the dragon in words, and send **real** references instead — the
brand package, the existing logo, the parchment print kit. Those are genuine work and they say
"this is a business that knows what it is," which is exactly the impression that gets a good
illustrator interested.

**If it comes up, be straight about it:**

> For what it's worth, I did mock something up with an AI tool just to work out what I wanted —
> happy to send it or not, whichever you'd prefer. I'm not looking for a copy of it; I want your
> version of the idea.

Offering the choice is the respectful move, and honesty costs nothing here. Hiding it and being
found out later costs the relationship.

## 3. What to send once they say yes

📎 **Attach `tools/print-kit/brand/brand-package.html` as a PDF.** Almost nobody DMs an illustrator
with a brand guide. It answers half their questions before they ask and makes the job look easy.

> **The piece:** a single dragon, front-facing-ish, that works as a logo.
>
> **Who she is:** green, friendly, clearly made of fabric — patchwork panels, visible stitching,
> maybe a patch or two. Small flowers in her horns. She is *sewing*: needle in hand, a scrap of
> embroidery on her lap. Storybook illustration, not a slick vector mascot.
>
> **Palette:** deep green `#4E5839`, sage `#938858`, wine `#7B322C`, on parchment `#f0ddbc`. All
> four are in the brand guide.
>
> **What I need delivered:**
> - The full illustration, **vector or 2000px+ with a transparent background**
> - **A simplified version that still reads at about 32px** — this becomes the favicon
> - **A one-colour (solid black) version** for a rubber stamp
> - **Full commercial rights / buyout**, in writing
>
> **Timing:** I'd like it by early January. I'm moving house through December, so I'll be slow to
> reply some weeks — I'd rather agree the sketch stage before then.
>
> **Not needed:** the wordmark. I have that.

⚠️ **The three deliverables in bold are the whole point** and most illustrators will not supply them
unless asked. A beautiful drawing that turns to mush at favicon size, or fills in solid on a stamp,
is not a logo — it is a picture.

## 3b. The public "illustrator wanted" post

*For **r/HungryArtists** and **r/forhire**, where you post the brief and artists come to you.
Both subs **require a budget in the post** and remove the ones without. The `[HIRING]` tag is
mandatory on r/forhire.*

**Title:**

> [HIRING] Character logo illustration — a hand-drawn patchwork dragon for a small handmade sewing
> business — $350 USD, full commercial buyout

**Body:**

> I run a small handmade sewing business — Dragon Ink and Thread. Totes, bows, pet bandanas, book
> sleeves. It's veteran-owned and it's just me. I'm rebranding the website in January and I want a
> proper character mark drawn for it.
>
> **The piece:** a friendly green dragon who is visibly made of fabric — patchwork panels, visible
> stitching, a patch or two — sitting and **sewing**, needle in hand, a scrap of embroidery on her
> lap. Small flowers in her horns. Storybook illustration rather than a slick vector mascot.
>
> **Deliverables:**
> - The full illustration — vector or 2000px+, transparent background
> - A **simplified version that still reads at about 32px** (it becomes the favicon)
> - A **one-colour solid black version** (it becomes a rubber stamp)
>
> **Rights:** full commercial buyout. It goes on the website, product labels, packaging and a
> stamp, and I'll put that in writing.
>
> **Budget:** $350 USD, negotiable upward for the right portfolio. Half up front, half on delivery.
>
> **Timeline:** brief mid-November, rough sketches before the end of the month, final by early
> January. I'm moving house through December so I'll be slow to reply some weeks — I'd rather
> agree the sketch stage before that starts.
>
> **Hand-drawn only, please. No AI-generated work.**
>
> **To apply:** drop a portfolio link and one line about why this one interests you. I have a brand
> guide — palette, type, the marks I already use — that I'll send to anyone I'm talking seriously
> with, so you won't be guessing at the style.

### Why it's shaped this way

- ⛔ **It asks for no free samples and no spec work.** Posts that do are downvoted, and rightly.
  A portfolio link is the whole application.
- ✅ **"Hand-drawn only, no AI" is worth saying.** It is true, it is what she wants, and on those
  subs it signals she is on their side. ⚠️ **It also means not leading with the AI reference** —
  see section 2.
- ✅ **Offering the brand guide is the differentiator.** Almost no hiring post comes with one, and
  it turns a vague brief into an easy job.
- ✅ **The budget is in the title.** Artists filter on it, so burying it costs replies.

### The short social version

*For her own Instagram or Facebook — different shape, and the tag-a-friend ask is the bit that
actually travels.*

> Looking for an illustrator 🐉
>
> I'm rebranding in January and I need a new dragon — hand-drawn, green, made of patchwork,
> sitting and sewing. Storybook, not slick.
>
> Paid commission, **$350**, full commercial rights, **hand-drawn only**. Brief in November,
> finished by early January.
>
> If that's your sort of thing, send me a portfolio link. **And if you know someone whose work
> would suit, tag them** — I'd much rather find the right person than the first person.

## 4. Before money moves

- **Deposit, not the full amount.** Half up front, half on delivery is normal and fair.
- **Agree the sketch stage.** One round of rough sketches before colour, so a wrong direction costs
  a day rather than the whole job.
- **Get the rights in writing** — an email saying so is enough. "Full commercial rights including
  use on products, packaging and printed material, transferred on final payment."
- **Ask what they want credited.** Many illustrators are happy without credit on the mark itself but
  like a tag on the announcement post. That costs nothing and buys goodwill.
