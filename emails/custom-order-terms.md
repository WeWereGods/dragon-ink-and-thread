# Custom order terms — reusable blocks

Copy for **quotes and invoices**, not emails. Pasted by hand into Stripe when a custom
order is invoiced. Lives here because `emails/` already holds the customer-facing custom-order
copy (`custom-orders-enquiry.md`), and because the repo — not Drive, not a chat log — is where
copy is written.

**How to use this:** take only the blocks the job actually needs. A disclaimer listing risks
that don't apply reads as boilerplate, and boilerplate gets skimmed. Three sharp sentences a
customer actually reads beat ten they don't.

⚠️ **Stripe's terms field is short in practice.** Aim for **500 characters**. Every block below
carries its length, and there is a worked example of combining several at the bottom.

---

## The blocks

### 1 · Irreversible alteration — *any job that cuts up the customer's own garments* (156)

> [GARMENTS] will be cut apart to make this piece and cannot be made back into clothing. By
> approving this invoice you confirm you understand and accept this.

**Never cut this one to save space.** It is the whole reason a memory piece can go wrong in a
way no refund fixes. Name the actual garments — "the jacket, trousers, shirt and pocket square"
lands; "your items" does not.

### 2 · Leftover material returned (63)

> Any cloth left over is returned to you with the finished piece.

Say *with the finished piece*, not just *returned*. Offcuts are the only spare material on
irreplaceable cloth, so they are held until the piece is done in case a section needs recutting.
Phrased as a delay it sounds like foot-dragging; phrased as a reason it is obviously in their
favour.

### 3 · Customer-supplied materials (121)

> You provide [WHAT THEY SUPPLY]; I provide [WHAT YOU SUPPLY]. I can't guarantee how materials
> I haven't supplied will age.

### 4 · Archival warning — *anything involving paper or photographs* (86)

> Please use acid-free board for anything on paper — ordinary board yellows photographs.

Pairs with block 3 and is the reason it exists. If the customer supplies the backing, their own
non-archival board can ruin their photographs years later — and the blame lands on the maker.
One sentence moves that risk where it belongs.

### 5 · Clock starts on arrival (47)

> The [N] days begins when everything reaches me.

⚠️ **The most-earned line in this file.** Five jobs in August 2026 slipped because a quote
started before the materials were in the house — two fabric deliveries, a bolt of ticking,
quilt batting, and a bag of bow clips. Quote from the date materials arrive, or don't start
the clock.

### 6 · Scope locked to the drawing (76)

> The price covers the design as drawn; changes must be agreed before cutting.

Requires an actual drawing (`designs/`). It is what stops a finished piece being compared to a
reference image nobody ever agreed to make, and it keeps a growing design a conversation rather
than a dispute.

### 7 · Final sale (74)

> Custom work is final sale, and payment is non-refundable once work begins.

⚠️ **Say "payment" when they pay in full, "the deposit" when they pay a deposit.** A clause
describing a payment structure you aren't using is exactly the sentence that gets argued over.

### 8 · Care — wool wall piece (120)

> This is a wall piece, not bedding: it is wool and cannot be washed. Keep out of direct
> sunlight; check for moths yearly.

📌 **Fuller care notes belong on a card that ships WITH the piece**, not in the invoice. Moths
and sun matter in the customer's hands years later, not in a document from the month they
ordered.

### 9 · Turnaround exceptions (70)

> Most custom pieces take 10–14 days. This one is [N], because [REASON].

⚠️ **A price band without its turnaround is half a promise.** Quilts are 8–12 weeks, not 10–14
days; heirloom pieces from tailored garments are longer again. Whenever an unusual turnaround
is quoted, the timing copy is part of the change, not a follow-up.

## Worked example — Linda's shadow box, 2026-08-19 (494)

Blocks 3 + 4 + 5 + 7, plus one line of description, combined:

> Your husband's costume and memorabilia, arranged and secured in the display box you supply.
> You provide the box, backing and fixings; I provide what holds each piece in place. Please use
> acid-free board for anything on paper — ordinary board yellows photographs, and I can't
> guarantee materials I haven't supplied. Mounting is reversible where possible; anything
> permanent is agreed first. The 30 days begins when everything reaches me. Final sale; payment
> is non-refundable once work begins.

What was **cut** to reach 500, and why it was safe: the detail about stitching and padding
(flavour), "handled with care but accepted as they are" (implied by final sale), and the
itemised supply list (better said in a message than buried in invoice terms).

## Descriptions

Terms protect; the description is what makes someone feel the piece was worth the money. Keep
it plain — on a memorial piece especially, restraint reads as respect and floridness reads as
sales. Name the real garments, the real dimensions, and what it is **for**.

The pattern that works: *what it is and what it's made from · how it's built · what it's for.*
End on the last one — "Made to hang and be looked at, not to be used as a quilt" does the work
of a care note and a scope boundary in one line.

⚠️ **Never derive a feature from a photograph.** Construction, dimensions and how a thing
attaches come from the maker. A photo proves a thing exists, not how it works — a "works two
ways" claim was written into copy in August 2026 off two photographs that turned out to be two
different totes.
